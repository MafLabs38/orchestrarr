# 0031 — A mandate is the signed permission to do a mission in a world

- **Status**: proposed (the architecture was described to the maintainer on 2026-11-13 and the maintainer agreed with it in broad lines; accepted when the pull request that carries it is merged)
- **Date**: 2026-11-13
- **Spec**: D-84 ([FUNCTIONAL.md](../FUNCTIONAL.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md))

## Context

Moving between worlds needs a way to say what an agent may do, where, for how long. Today the approval of a decision card plays part of that role for fixed tasks. The maintainer wants the agent to **ask for a mandate** to enter the next world, and a second agent to grant it.

## Decision

1. A **mandate** is issued by the control plane for **one world and one mission**. A merge act names **the base branch and the head commit** it covers (proposed in SPEC Q-29, part 2, and adopted here). It states: the world and the place, the repositories and branches or the environment, the **acts allowed**, the frame (paths allowed and forbidden, network), the **budget**, the **expiry**, and who issued it.
2. It is **signed with an asymmetric key** whose private half is held **only by the control plane**; each runner holds the public half and **refuses** a mandate that is not valid, expired, revoked, or **issued for another runner or another world** (**the mission runner**: the one that accepts the mandate, starts the session and consumes the nonce). A mandate is **single-use for starting**: its nonce is recorded when a runner accepts it, and a runner that restarts resumes the **session** it recorded (a session id in its own store), within the mandate's validity, but cannot start it again. A send-back continues in the same session under the same mandate until it expires or its budget is used. Each **gated act** a mandate lists is **recorded when it is performed**, so that the same act is not performed twice by replaying the request and the runner checks the kill switch and revocation before every gated act. A key shared with the runners would let whoever takes a staging runner forge a release mandate. **A mandate names its mission runner and the act gateways that may perform its gated acts, each by identity and with the acts it may do.** A gateway accepts a mandate only if it is named in it, **never starts a session on it and never consumes its nonce**; it requires that the request is **signed by the mission runner the mandate names** (a key pinned in the gateway's own configuration) and carries **the authority's signed answer that the mandate was counted**, or asks the authority itself, and it records each act it performs under the nonce of the mandate and an act id, so that the runner's session and the gateway agree and an act is not performed twice. The merge identity has one holder, the act gateway of the Git world. For a repository that deploys by merge (GitOps, publication on merge) the release mission's mandate goes to the release runner, which **starts and drives the worker's sandbox on a separate host, performs the verification, and holds the deployment credentials outside every agent sandbox**; the merge that deploys is a gated act that the release runner asks the Git-world gateway to perform, under the same certificate, authorised at the act; a revert is not an act of its own, it is a pull request of a new repair (ADR 0040).
   The **baseline mission** of ADR 0034 has no problem and no plan entry: its mandate is issued by the scheduler under a **system standing rule** that the maintainer created, limited to the staging world.
3. The **first mandate** of a problem (or the system rule above) comes from the plan (validated by the maintainer, or covered by a standing rule). The following ones come **only after the controller has crossed** the previous mission (ADR 0032).
4. A mandate **cannot be widened** by its holder; a change of scope is a new mandate. It **expires** and can be **revoked** by the kill switch, the maintainer or the budget.
5. It **reuses what exists**: a plan validation is an approval with the second factor, a standing rule is a delegation, the journal and the signature checks stay.
6. Every mandate, refusal and crossing is written to the audit trail.
7. **The maintainer's approval is proved to the runner, not only asserted by the control plane** (ADR 0017 point 5 is kept, the double control stays): the plan validation, and the creation or widening of a standing rule, are certified by an **approval authority**, a service apart from the control plane with its own key, which **holds the request, shows it to the maintainer on its own channel, checks the code typed there with the second-factor guard**, and signs a certificate of exactly that request: the problem or rule, the application, the repositories, the highest world, the budget, the forbidden paths, **how many mandates it may back** and the expiry. Its own policy bounds the life of a certificate and the number of mandates, and a **standing rule's certificate may reach the release world** (the maintainer, 2026-11-17, SPEC D-94: the automation goes as far as it is equipped to go), **once three safeguards exist**: the authority's own channel (Q-36), FR-MAN-1c (each mandate under the rule bound to its finding and checked by the runner) and a second verifier of the escalation of a failed release under a rule; until then the authority's policy caps a rule at the Git world (the policy is a launch contract file), and the cap is lifted **per rule**, with the maintainer's code on the authority's own channel and an entry in the audit trail; a release runner refuses a release-world mandate under a rule that has no binding it checked itself, and the binding is recorded at the problem's first mandate so that a repair is checked against that record. The rule's bounds are its class, its count and its expiry. A release that failed under a rule is escalated, nobody having approved that problem (proposed, FR-WLD-13; the maintainer to confirm). A mandate is accepted by a runner only if it falls within a certificate (the authority's signature, the validity, this problem or rule, the application and class, the acts of the world, the forbidden paths), the runner asks the authority, before it accepts a mandate, to **count** it against its certificate (**one counter for every runner, held by the authority**: a count kept by each runner would let a compromised control plane spread mandates over the runners), and refuses one whose certificate the authority **withdrew**; the counter and the withdrawals are kept by the authority in a store that a restart keeps, and **a runner that cannot ask the authority refuses**, as it does when the stop cannot be read. **The answers are authenticated**: the authority signs each answer (a count or a withdrawal) with its key, bound to the runner's request (a nonce chosen by the runner, the certificate, the mandate's nonce) and to a short validity, so that nothing on the path, the control plane included, can answer in its place or replay an old answer; the authority's address and key are in the runner's own configuration, never given by the control plane; only an authenticated runner may count a use, and only the maintainer, on the authority's own channel, may withdraw a certificate. The control plane reads the withdrawals too: a withdrawn certificate stops the crossings, the acts and the sweep of the problems that run on it. A compromised control plane can therefore sign mandates **inside** what the maintainer validated, not beyond it. **Limits that remain, stated**: the escalation of a failed release under a standing rule is applied by the conductor only, and the cap of the authority's default policy at the Git world is what holds it until a second verifier of that escalation exists, which is a requirement of the vertical that lifts the cap: that vertical designs it (what it records, from which channel, what it does when the record is missing), and the cap is not lifted before it; until the plan service uses the authority's own channel, the code is not bound to the content (the limit of ADR 0017), so that channel is a prerequisite of any certificate that reaches the release world or of any standing rule; a standing rule binds a class, not a problem, so under a rule **each mandate is bound to the finding, or the pull request and its head commit, that the runner checks itself** (FR-MAN-1c); and the authority on the same host as the control plane protects against a bug, not against its compromise (another host, or a hardware-backed key).
8. **An act is authorised when it is done, not only when it is asked for**: a mandate says which acts may be asked for. The **act gateway** of the world (the runner that holds the identity for the merge or the deployment, apart from every agent sandbox) verifies the mandate and the certificate itself and asks the control plane to authorise the act now: the power read at this moment, the stop, the facts on the exact head commit and base branch (the diff, the pipelines, the files the pipeline executes, whether the merge may deploy, the tags, the secrets) and the audit entry written first. Where a merge may deploy, it is a release-world act. A revert is a pull request like any other, never an act of its own, and no rule of a protected branch is bypassed ([ADR 0040](0040-the-way-back-is-a-change-the-loop-makes.md)). **The merge is bound to the checked commit**: the call carries the expected head, and where the forge allows it the gateway merges a branch that only it can write, created at the checked commit. The control plane's own credential on the forge is read-only (D-63).

## Consequences

- The approval, the delegation and the journal are adapted, not rebuilt.
- The checks that code can make are made by code (the plan covers the problem, the budget, the kill switch, the staging requirement, the diff touches no forbidden file, CI is green on the exact commit); the controller judges the rest.
